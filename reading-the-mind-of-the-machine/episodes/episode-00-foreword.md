# Reading the Mind of the Machine -- Episodes One to Fourteen

*A Course in Mechanistic Interpretability, with its Supplement, bound as one*

*Being the seven episodes of the course and the seven of its supplement, in a single volume. The course proper -- Episodes One to Seven -- reads the finished machine along the single forward pass: the residual stream and the geometry of features, attention read closely, the induction heads and the origin of in-context learning, superposition and the toy models, the sparse autoencoder in full, the discipline of circuits and intervention, and the stakes of steering and editing. The supplement -- Episodes Eight to Fourteen -- is the reader’s refusal to leave the harder questions at the level of a promise: Eight and Nine descend to the engine room, the transcoder and the attribution graph it draws; Ten through Twelve turn from how the finished machine runs to how it grew, how it reasons across the tokens it writes, and what survives when one mind is copied into another; Thirteen and Fourteen carry the descent into post-training and into practice -- the machinery of approval by which these minds are finished, and the meeting of this science with the craft of evaluating what it studies.*

*This printing is the Revised Second Edition. In July of 2026 the whole season went back under review: all fourteen episodes -- the course proper and the supplement alike -- were re-examined against the literature and revised, their citations verified to the page, their cross-references and honesty caveats repaired. Episode Seven’s gallery of climactic evidence now declares openly the borrowed instruments -- the transcoder, the attribution graph -- on which it rests, and which the supplement then builds properly. And the season is no longer alone: it is accompanied by a sibling work, the Desk Volume -- fourteen chapters of formal statements, derivations, protocols, and tiered problems, with its Bench of laboratory exercises -- where the arguments the ear can only sketch are set down in full. What earlier printings called the Revised and Expanded Edition is folded into this one; every episode here stands as revised, none merely as first delivered.*
