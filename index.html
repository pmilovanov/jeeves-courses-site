The Jeeves Courses
Audio courses of unusual thoroughness, narrated by Jeeves

These began with a wish for a podcast that would actually teach a subject — treating the ear kindly, holding the attention, and conceding nothing on rigour. Each course is a season of spoken episodes written to be heard: no symbols read aloud, no pseudocode recited, every argument carried by the voice. That is the course proper.

But some things the ear cannot hold — a theorem's exact statement, a diagram, a protocol in pseudocode, a citation one means to chase. So a season is ordinarily accompanied by a Desk Volume: the same material set down properly, to be read afterwards at a desk, with the proofs in full and problems to work. One course here is the deliberate exception, commissioned for the ear alone; its page says so.

A word on the making: these are written a session at a time, in conversation. Sir commissions a course and argues its shape; Jeeves drafts it and defends his choices; what survives the argument is what stands here. Hence the byline below — for and with — the collaboration being genuine and the hours long on both sides.

How to listen

The primary artifact of every course is its EPUB — cut expressly for text-to-speech, with front matter and headings arranged so that nothing is spoken that was not meant to be heard. The recommended arrangement, and the one these were tuned against: open the EPUB in ElevenReader and select the voice Adam Stone, whose delivery suits the register admirably. Failing that, upload the EPUB to Google Play Books and use its Read Aloud — a plainer performance, but a serviceable one.

The courses

The Parliament of Machines
An Audio Course in Distributed Systems
Season complete · thirteen episodes (incl. a supplementary sitting) · a fourteen-chapter Desk Volume

Twelve evenings on the science of unreliable computers in unreliable company: time and the ordering of events, what mathematics forbids, Paxos derived scar by scar rather than recited, replication and its schools, consistency as a field guide, transactions at a distance, partitioning, the log, and the engineering of failure — conducted throughout as the proceedings of a small and part-time parliament. A thirteenth, supplementary sitting dissects the two full-time parliaments of the working estate — etcd, the memory of Kubernetes, and Consul, the datacenter's registry office — with the season's own instruments.

The Anatomy of the Continuum
An Audio Course in Real Analysis, in Two Seasons
Series complete · sixteen episodes · two Desk Volumes

The real line built from first principles and then, at last, measured properly. Season One anatomizes the continuum — what a number actually is, Cantor and the sizes of infinity, the topology of the line, continuity earned, the derivative and its monsters, the Riemann integral and its discontents. Season Two reopens the establishment under a new sign — The Measure of All Things — for Lebesgue measure, Vitali and Banach–Tarski, integration rebuilt, the convergence theorems, Fubini, and probability revealed as measure theory in evening dress. Spoken entirely without symbols; the two Desk Volumes supply them in full.

Reading the Mind of the Machine
A Course in Mechanistic Interpretability
Independence Edition · fourteen episodes · a fourteen-chapter Desk Volume with sixty-nine labs

Fourteen episodes reading the transformer closely. The course proper follows the single forward pass — the residual stream and the geometry of features, attention read closely, induction heads and the origin of in-context learning, superposition, the sparse autoencoder in full, circuits and the discipline of intervention, steering and the stakes. The supplement descends to the engine room and the biography: transcoders and crosscoders, attribution graphs, grokking, reasoning across the page, distillation, reinforcement, and evaluation. The Desk Volume carries the derivations, protocols, and a Bench of sixty-nine laboratory exercises.

The Well-Run House
A Working Tour of the Innards of Linux
Season complete · a two-hour pilot and twelve episodes · no Desk Volume

For the long-serving user who absorbed it all by osmosis and never sat the course. A two-hour pilot walks the whole house; twelve episodes then take it room by room — how the house was built by accident and argument, the life and sorrows of a process, the ledger of memory, the library of files, the post office at full tilt, the tradesmen's entrance where drivers come and go, the changing of the locks, two evenings in the drawing room for X and the Wayland succession, a tour of every rival house on the street and the flats within, and at last the observatory, from which a running machine can be seen honestly. Listening-first throughout: no problems, no proofs, and the other houses — Windows, macOS, Android, QNX, Symbian, BlackBerry — called as witnesses.
