Integration Rebuilt

The Measure of All Things, Season Two, Episode Four

The Merchant's Till

Good day, sir.

The image is Lebesgue's own, from his own pen, and since the entire evening unfolds from it I shall give it the opening it deserves. A merchant closes his shop and counts the day's takings. He may count the coins one by one, in the order the till received them — a penny, then a florin, then another penny, then a shilling — keeping a running total that lurches by whatever denomination fortune supplies next. That, sir, is Riemann's way: march through the domain in its given order, left to right along the interval, accepting whatever values the function hands you at each step. Or the merchant may first sort the till — all the pennies in one pile, all the florins in another — then count each pile once and multiply: so many pennies at a penny each, so many florins at two shillings. That is Lebesgue's way: partition the range, not the domain. Fix a value, ask how much of the domain attains it — or nearly attains it — weigh that set of ground with the measure this season has built, and sum value times ground over the piles.

Notice what the sorted count requires that the sequential count does not: the ability to weigh a pile. "How much ground takes the value near two shillings" is a question about the size of a set — an arbitrary set, as wild as the function is wild — and for sixty years that question had no answer, which is why Riemann's way was the only way. The whole of this season to date — the outer measure, the tribe, the contract, even the ghosts — was the building of the weighing-house. Tonight the merchant walks in, and the count begins.

The programme, sir. First, the functions that can be sorted at all — the measurable functions, the tribe's own function class, and their supreme virtue: they are stable under every limit this course has ever taken, the stability continuity conspicuously lacked. Second, the atoms of the new integral — the simple functions — and the staircase of the range, the construction that realizes the merchant's sorting. Third, the integral itself, assembled in three stages, and the bell has an engagement at the second. Fourth, the phrase this course has owed you since the first season's finale reaches full working order: almost everywhere. Then the ceremonies, in procession: the static tamed — act three, booked since eighteen twenty-nine by Dirichlet himself; the old regime absorbed — every Riemann verdict preserved; and the prophecy proved — Lebesgue's criterion, on credit these four episodes, discharged in full, closing the vocabulary crisis that ended Season One. And at the last, a new resident: the devil's staircase, the dust's final contracted return, bearing two strange gifts. To work.

Functions That Can Be Sorted

The sorted count weighs the pile at each value, so the piles must be weighable: for every threshold, the set of ground where the function exceeds the threshold must be a citizen of the tribe. That is the definition, sir, in one breath: a function is measurable when, for every number a, the set of points where the function's value exceeds a is a measurable set. The pile above every waterline, weighable. Equivalent formulations — at least a, below a, at most a — come free by complements and countable intersections, the tribe's own operations; the desk drills the equivalences. And the definition immediately swallows everything you might hope: continuous functions are measurable — the pile above a waterline is the preimage of an open half-line, open by Season One's topological characterization of continuity, and opens are citizens; step functions, monotone functions, the static, Thomae's creature, the indicators of citizens — measurable to the last specimen; the desk runs the parade. To manufacture a non-measurable function one must conjure — the indicator of Vitali's delegation is the canonical ghost — so the class is, for the practitioner, everything.

Pause on the definition's shape, sir, for it rhymes across the seasons and the rhyme is load-bearing. Season One characterized continuity topologically: preimages of open sets are open. Tonight's definition is the same sentence sung in the new key: preimages of half-lines — and thence, the desk shows, of every Borel set — are measurable. Continuity is compatibility with the topology; measurability is compatibility with the tribe; and every structural theorem tonight flows from choosing the right notion of compatibility for a theory of weighing. One convention completes the class: the functions may take the values plus and minus infinity — the limit stability about to be proved manufactures infinite suprema whether we like them or not — and the tribe absorbs them without protest: the set where a function is infinite is a countable intersection of above-the-waterline piles, weighable like everything else. The desk seats the conventions; the honest cases where infinities matter are flagged as they arise.

The algebra is equally obliging — sums, products, absolute values, maxima and minima of measurable functions are measurable, with proofs that are two-line pile-bookkeeping; the desk files them. One caution flag, planted now and detonated at the staircase: composition. A continuous function of a measurable function is measurable; a measurable function of a continuous function, sir, can fail — and the failure is tonight's closing exhibit, not a footnote. Do not compose casually in this subject; the desk's caution box states the safe direction.

But the crown of the class — the property the whole evening leans on, the property I have been promising since Season One's Episode Seven taught you to fear limits of functions — is this. Take any sequence of measurable functions. Its pointwise supremum is measurable. Its infimum is measurable. Its limsup and liminf — measurable. And if the sequence converges pointwise, the limit function is measurable. Every limiting operation this course has ever performed on functions, the class absorbs without condition. Compare, sir — and the comparison is the point — the continuous functions, whose pointwise limits shattered into the static and the monsters and Cauchy's corpse in Season One: continuity survives only uniform convergence, purchased at a premium; measurability survives everything, gratis. The proof is the season's grammar made flesh, and it fits in the ear: the points where the supremum exceeds a are exactly the points where some function of the sequence exceeds a — a countable union of weighable piles, weighable by the tribe's third clause. Limsup is an infimum of suprema — countable operations stacked twice; the limit, where it exists, is the limsup. The sigma in sigma-algebra, sir, was purchased precisely so that this paragraph could be true. Limits are home. That is what the retreat to the tribe bought, and tonight the purchase pays out.

The Atoms

The merchant's piles, formalized. A simple function is one taking finitely many values, each on a measurable set — a finite measurable staircase: value a-one on the citizen A-one, a-two on A-two, and so on, finitely many storeys. Indicators of citizens are the one-storey cases; every simple function is a finite weighted stack of indicators. These are the atoms of the theory, and their integral writes itself, for it is the merchant's multiplication: the integral of a simple function is the sum, over its storeys, of value times the measure of the ground carrying that value. So many florins times the florin. One clerical theorem stands behind the formula — a simple function wears many representations, and all price identically; the desk proves the well-definedness with the standard refinement — and the atoms' arithmetic follows: linearity and monotonicity on simple functions, proved at the desk by common-refinement bookkeeping, Season One's oldest architecture in its newest dress.

Your trade has met the atoms, sir, under an alias: a simple function is precisely a discrete random variable — finitely many outcomes, each on a measurable event — and the merchant's multiplication is precisely its expectation, value times probability, summed. When Episode Eight unmasks expectation as tonight's integral, the atoms will already be old acquaintances. One convention keeps the unbounded honest: values times measures may meet infinity — a positive value on infinite ground prices infinite, and zero times infinite ground prices zero; the season's extended arithmetic, one prohibition still standing, handles the books. The desk states the conventions once; they will not surprise us again.

The Staircase of the Range

Now the construction that makes the metaphor an algorithm, sir — the bridge from atoms to arbitrary functions, and I shall narrate it fully, because it is the merchant's sorting performed before your eyes.

Take a nonnegative measurable function — the general case splits into positive and negative parts presently. Fix a resolution: at stage n, slice the range — the values, not the domain — into dyadic denominations of width one over two to the n, from nought up to the ceiling n. Assign each point of the domain the largest denomination not exceeding its value: if the function's value at x lies between three-eighths and four-eighths at resolution eight, the point x is binned at three-eighths. The result is a simple function — finitely many denominations, each on a pile that is weighable precisely because the function is measurable: each bin is a difference of two above-the-waterline sets. And the stages improve monotonically: refine the resolution and raise the ceiling, and each point's assigned denomination climbs — never falls — toward the true value. At every point, the staircase of assignments converges upward to the function; where the function is bounded, the convergence is uniform once the ceiling clears the bound, with error pinned under one denomination.

Let the theorem be said plainly, for it is the structure theorem of the whole subject: every nonnegative measurable function is the increasing pointwise limit of simple functions. Every one, sir — the static, the monsters, the unbounded, anything the tribe can weigh — is a limit of merchant's staircases climbing from below. Season One built functions from power series and uniform limits and mourned what those could not reach; the new theory builds every measurable function whatsoever from finite staircases, in one uniform construction. The desk writes the two-line verification that the bins are citizens and the climb is monotone; the picture — the range sliced ever finer, the staircase hugging the function from below — is the one to keep.

The Integral in Three Stages

The integral now assembles itself, in three stages, each stage a single idea.

Stage one is behind us: simple functions price by the merchant's multiplication — value times ground, summed over the storeys.

Stage two, the heart. For a nonnegative measurable function, define the integral as the supremum, over all simple functions squeezed between nought and the function, of their simple integrals — the best sorted count achievable from below. And there, sir — the bell. Ring the sixteenth. The definition is a supremum over an infinite inventory of staircases, and the number exists by the completeness axiom and by nothing else — the same axiom that manufactured the Darboux ledgers at rings thirteen and fourteen, now manufacturing their successor. Mark the echo and the difference, both deliberate: Riemann's lower ledger was a supremum over partitions of the domain; Lebesgue's integral is a supremum over staircases of the range; and where Riemann needed his lower ledger to meet an upper one — two books, agreement required, functions refused when the books stood apart — Lebesgue's definition needs no second ledger at all. One supremum, defined for every nonnegative measurable function without exception, infinite when the mass is infinite and finite when it is not, refusing nothing. The asymmetry is the design, sir: measurability has already done the vetting that Riemann's second ledger was doing by hand, and the staircase theorem guarantees the supremum is approached by honest constructions rather than hoped at from a distance. Flagged for your veto as ever; the register inks ring sixteen at the integral's birth.

Let the machine compute once in the open, sir, before the third stage — the schoolroom's simplest integral, re-derived by sorting. Take the identity function on the unit interval; the old regime prices it at one half, area of a triangle. The merchant's count at resolution n: slice the range into dyadic denominations of width one over two to the n; the pile assigned denomination k over two to the n is the set of points whose value — which is the point itself — lies in that slice: an interval of width one over two to the n. Value times ground, summed: the sum over k of, k over two to the n, times, one over two to the n — a finite arithmetic series the desk evaluates in one line — equal to one half minus one over two to the n plus one. The staircases' prices climb to one half exactly as the resolution refines; the supremum is one half; the new integral agrees with the old on the first specimen ever integrated, and you have watched the range-partition actually count. The general agreement theorem is tonight's absorption ceremony; the specimen was the handshake.

Stage three, the bookkeeping. A general measurable function splits canonically into its positive part and its negative part — the function's gains and losses, each nonnegative, each measurable, summing back by subtraction. Integrate each part by stage two. When both integrals are finite — equivalently, when the integral of the absolute value is finite — the function is called integrable, and its integral is gains minus losses: lawful subtraction, both terms finite, the season's one prohibition respected to the letter. When exactly one part is infinite the integral is honestly plus or minus infinity; when both are infinite the integral is undefined — infinity minus infinity, never written. Three stages, sir: multiply, climb, subtract. The machine is built.

Its first manners, all desk-proved tonight within the estate the proofs can honestly reach: monotonicity — larger functions price larger; the simple cases of linearity; and the frame conventions for integrating over a citizen — restrict, or multiply by the indicator, the two agreeing. One credit I flag now, plainly, because the honesty clause governs even architecture: full additivity of the integral — that the integral of a sum is the sum of the integrals, for arbitrary nonnegative measurable functions — is stated tonight and proved next week, where it will fall out of the first crown jewel in one line. The desk proves it tonight for the bounded estate — bounded functions on finite ground — which is every case tonight's ceremonies require; the unbounded credit is the construction's last, and the register logs it with a one-week maturity. I decline to pretend the machine is fully certified before the convergence theorems certify it; next week, sir, the papers complete themselves.

Almost Everywhere, At Work

Debt eleven's final instalment falls due, and I shall pay it as a working demonstration rather than a definition, since the definition has been assembling itself for three weeks.

A property holds almost everywhere when the set of points where it fails is null. The phrase entered as prophecy in Season One's finale; the sets were weighed in this season's first evening; the amnesty of the negligible — every subset of a null set a citizen — was banked at the completion. Tonight the phrase goes to work on functions, and the two working lemmas are these. First: if two functions agree almost everywhere, their integrals agree — the disagreement set is null, contributes nothing to any staircase's price, and the desk makes the bookkeeping exact. Second, the completion earning its keep: a function agreeing almost everywhere with a measurable function is itself measurable — the tribe cannot tell them apart, because the completion already granted amnesty to every fragment of the null disagreement. Together, sir, the lemmas license the liberation the plan promised: for every purpose of integration, functions equal almost everywhere are the same function. The theory works with functions-up-to-negligible-disagreement — equivalence classes, if one wants the formal costume — and pointwise pedantry retires. What happens on a null set is, for the integral, clerical noise. Season One agonized over single points of misbehaviour; the new regime files them under amnesty and moves on.

Two demonstrations fix the dialect's reach. Modify any function on the rationals — every rational, all of them at once — and no integral notices: the modification set is null, and the piles reweigh to the gram. Season One spent an entire evening distinguishing functions that differ at a single point — the cornered ramp's one bad address, the removable discontinuities, the anxieties of pointwise behaviour; the new regime prices all such distinctions at exactly nought. And the anxiety that powered Season One's Episode Seven — pointwise limits destroy continuity, so limits must be policed uniformly — loses its sting for integration entire: the limit-stability theorem keeps every pointwise limit measurable, and next week's theorems will price the limits too. The uniform premium, sir, was continuity's bill; the tribe never owed it.

The Taming of the Static

And now, sir, the ceremony the course has carried for two seasons. The static — Dirichlet's function, one on the rationals, zero on the irrationals. Born in Season One, Episode Six, christened as pure interference. Act two, the season finale: it defeated the Riemann integral utterly — both ledgers pinned apart at every partition, refusal absolute, and I asked you then to remember how total the defeat was. Act three was booked for tonight, and I shall not stretch it, because the entire point — the wonder beat of the evening, and I spend the budget here deliberately — is how short it is.

The static is a simple function. Two storeys: value one on the rationals — a citizen, measure zero; value nought on the irrationals — a citizen, measure one. The merchant's multiplication: one times nought, plus nought times one. The integral of the static is zero.

That is the taming entire, sir. One line — I promised you one line in Season One's finale, and there it stands. The function that broke the classical theory in open court is priced by the new one before the clerk has inked his pen: the static equals zero almost everywhere, and the amnesty does the rest. And the diagnosis, now visible in full: the static was never a difficult function. It is one of the simplest functions in the building — two values, two citizens. What was difficult was the domain-marching count, which stumbled at every step over interleaved denominations it had no way to sort. Sort the till, and the interference resolves into two tidy piles, one of them weightless. The problem was never the noise, sir; it was the bookkeeping — and the bell that rang at ring sixteen is the sound of the bookkeeping being done right.

The Old Regime, Absorbed

The second ceremony mirrors Chapter Two's absorption of Jordan, as I rehearsed twice, and completes it one storey up. Every function the old regime could integrate, the new regime integrates, to the same value: a Riemann-integrable function on a closed interval is Lebesgue measurable and Lebesgue integrable, and the two integrals agree exactly. Every schoolroom computation, every table of integrals, every fundamental-theorem evaluation from Season One's summit transfers into the new theory unaltered — the revolution, once again, expropriates no one.

One boundary post, planted honestly before the handshake: the absorption covers the proper Riemann integral — bounded functions, closed bounded intervals, Season One's honest core. The improper extensions do not all transfer: a conditionally convergent improper integral — one whose convergence leans on cancellation between infinite gains and infinite losses — is precisely a function whose absolute value carries infinite mass, and stage three's integrability clause refuses it, on the same grounds the rearrangement theorem of Season One convicted conditionally convergent series: cancellation-dependent totals are order-dependent totals, and the sorted count has no order to lean on. The desk states the standard specimen and the moral: Lebesgue absorbs everything Riemann proved, and declines some things Riemann merely got away with. The proof strategy narrates cleanly, sir, and it is Season One shaking hands with Season Two. Riemann integrability supplies squeezing staircases: step functions below and above with Darboux ledgers converging — and step functions are simple functions, the old regime's atoms a special case of the new. The squeezing steps converge, off a null set, to the function itself — the desk conducts the argument through the bounded estate's two-ledger machinery — whence the function is measurable by tonight's amnesty lemma, trapped between staircases whose prices agree, and the new integral is squeezed onto the old value. Absorption by squeeze, with the null set absorbing the exceptions: the proof, sir, is almost impertinently in-character for this season.

The Prophecy, Proved

And the third ceremony, sir: debt ten. At Season One's finale I read you a theorem from the future — Lebesgue's criterion, quoted as prophecy because its central phrase had no meaning: a bounded function on a closed interval is Riemann integrable precisely when its set of discontinuities has measure zero. The phrase was given meaning in this season's first hour; the machinery for the proof was forged the same evening — the oscillation gauge, the anatomy of the quiet sets; and tonight the proof is delivered at the desk in full, discharging the debt on the schedule fixed at issue. The vocabulary crisis of Season One — the static refused, Thomae admitted, no word for why — is formally closed.

The proof's two directions narrate in a breath apiece, and both run on this season's first evening. If the function is Riemann integrable, Riemann's own oscillation criterion — the starred problem of Season One's final chapter, now cashed — confines the loud points, those oscillating above any fixed threshold, to finite unions of intervals of arbitrarily small total length: each loud set is null, and the full discontinuity set is a countable union of loud sets — null by the ideal's second law. Conversely, if the discontinuities are null: fix a tolerance; the loud set at that tolerance is closed — the gauge's grain, proved in this season's first hour — and bounded, hence compact, Season One's conserved finiteness; being null and compact it hides under finitely many small intervals; and off those intervals the function oscillates quietly, so a partition fine within the quiet region and thrifty over the loud cover pins the Darboux ledgers together. Compactness converts the null cover to an auditable committee — the same conversion, sir, that priced the interval in this season's first evening, now run in the opposite direction. The auditor stamps; the criterion holds.

Read the old exhibits table one final time, with every verdict now a theorem: continuous functions — defect set empty, integrable. The staircases — countable defects, null, integrable. Thomae — defects exactly the rationals, null, integrable, integral zero. The dust's indicator — defects exactly the dust, null by this season's decree, integrable. The static — defects everywhere, measure one, refused. One criterion, no exceptions, every line proved. The vocabulary crisis is not merely resolved, sir; it is dissolved — the mystery was a missing instrument, and the instrument has been built, calibrated, and applied to its own motivating cases. I note for the register that Season One's finale promised precisely this closure, at precisely this address, and the delivery is on time to the episode.

The Devil's Staircase

One resident remains to be introduced, and the dust's long contract closes with it — the stranger return, promised in Season One, Episode Three, deferred through the paradox lounge and the measurement, delivered tonight in its final form.

Build a function on the unit interval alongside the dust's own construction. On the first removed middle third, let the function hold the constant value one half. On the two middles removed at stage two, the values one quarter and three quarters. On the four stage-three middles, the odd eighths — one eighth, three eighths, five eighths, seven eighths — and so on forever: on each removed interval, the dyadic value that splits the difference of its neighbours. The assignments are consistent, monotone, and defined on the dust's complement — measure one of the interval; extend to the dust itself by taking suprema, and anchor the endpoints at nought and one. The result is the Cantor function, sir — the trade's affectionate name is the devil's staircase — and its portfolio of properties reads like a dare. It is continuous on the whole interval: the dyadic values interleave so tightly that no jump survives, and the desk certifies continuity by the monotone-plus-dense-range argument, no epsilon unturned. It is nondecreasing, climbing from nought at the left endpoint to one at the right. And it is locally constant on the dust's complement — flat on every removed interval, flat, that is, almost everywhere. Its derivative exists and equals zero at almost every point of the interval; it accomplishes its entire ascent — a full unit of climb — on the dust, a set of measure zero. A continuous function that climbs from nought to one while flat almost everywhere: the season's two smallnesses taught you that sets can be large in number and null in land; the staircase is the function-shaped incarnation, all its motion compressed into no land at all.

And the staircase bears two gifts for the register, both desk-delivered tonight, both strange. First, the composition caution detonates: shift the staircase by adding the identity — the result is a strictly increasing continuous bijection of the unit interval onto an interval of length two, and it stretches the dust from measure zero to measure one. — and the stretching audits cleanly, sir: the shift preserves each removed bead's length, the beads total measure one, the image interval has length two, and additivity leaves the dust's image holding the missing unit: measure one exactly, from measure nought. Feed that image to the ghost factory of last week — a positive-measure set harbours a non-measurable subset — and pull the ghost back through the bijection: the preimage sits inside the dust, null, hence a measurable citizen by amnesty; yet its forward image is the ghost. The indicator bookkeeping then produces a measurable function of a continuous function that is not measurable — the caution flag of the evening's first section, cashed as a theorem. Second, and quieter: that same pulled-back citizen cannot be a Borel set — a homeomorphism carries Borel sets to Borel sets, and its image is a ghost, which no Borel set is — so the desk exhibits, as concretely as Choice permits, a Lebesgue-measurable set that is not Borel: the veil of Chapter Two's count, no longer a cardinality argument but a specimen in hand, one conjuring back. The devil's staircase, sir: flat almost everywhere, climbing a unit, stretching dust into land, and smuggling ghosts through continuity. The dust's account closes with its strangest instrument, and I invite you to notice that every one of its strangenesses is priced calmly by tonight's machine — the staircase integrates, by monotone absorption, to exactly one half, the desk showing the symmetry argument. The new regime, sir, does not merely tolerate the gallery of monsters; it does arithmetic on them.
