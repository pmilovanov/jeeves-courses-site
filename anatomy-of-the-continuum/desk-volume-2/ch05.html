5.2 The Climbing License

Theorem 5.1 (Monotone convergence — Levi, 1906).

Let $f_n : E \to [0, \infty]$ be measurable with $f_n \le f_{n+1}$ pointwise and $f_n \to f$ pointwise. Then $f$ is measurable and

\[ \int_E f_n \, dm \ \uparrow\ \int_E f \, dm . \]

(A.e. monotonicity and convergence suffice: patch the null set, Proposition 4.16.)

Proof.

$f$ is measurable as a pointwise limit (Theorem 4.6). Free half: $f_n \le f$ gives $\int f_n \le \int f$ (manners); the integrals climb (monotonicity again), so $L = \lim_n \int f_n$ exists in $[0, \infty]$ with $L \le \int f$.

Substance. Fix a simple $\varphi$ with $0 \le \varphi \le f$ and a discount $c \in (0, 1)$; write $\varphi = \sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$. The sufficiency sets

\[ E_n \ =\ \{ x \in E : f_n(x) \ge c\,\varphi(x) \} \]

are citizens ($f_n - c\varphi$ measurable), swell with $n$ (the climb), and exhaust $E$: where $\varphi(x) = 0$ membership is immediate; where $\varphi(x) > 0$, $c \varphi(x) < \varphi(x) \le f(x)$, so some $f_n(x)$ has cleared $c \varphi(x)$. Then

\[ \int f_n \ \ge\ \int_{E_n} f_n \ \ge\ c \int_{E_n} \varphi \ =\ c \sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i \, m(A_i \cap E_n) \ \xrightarrow[n \to \infty]{}\ c \sum_{i=1}^{N} a_i \, m(A_i) \ =\ c \int \varphi, \]

the arrow by the crankshaft (continuity along swelling chains, Proposition 1.3(i)) applied to each of the finitely many storeys $A_i \cap E_n \uparrow A_i$. Hence $L \ge c \int \varphi$ for every $c < 1$: $L \ge \int \varphi$ (let the discount expire); and taking the supremum over under-staircases, $L \ge \int f$ by ring sixteen’s definition. With the free half, $L = \int f$.

□
Remark 5.2 (The sign clause; the improper absorption).

Signed climbing fails: $f_n = -\tfrac1x \mathbf{1}_{(0, 1/n)}$-style descending starts have $\int f_n = -\infty$ throughout while the limit vanishes; a finite first floor restores the mirror (Problem I.8, the descending tower). Absorption: for measurable $f \ge 0$ on $(0, 1]$ with improper Riemann integral finite, the truncations $f \mathbf{1}_{[1/n, 1]}$ climb to $f$; each truncated integral is Riemann’s (Chapter 4’s absorption on the frame); MCT passes the limit — e.g. $\int_{(0,1]} x^{-1/2} \, dm = \lim_n (2 - 2/\sqrt{n}) = 2$. Chapter 4’s boundary post, absolutely convergent half: retired.

Corollary 5.3 (Additivity — debt fourteen discharged).

For measurable $f, g \ge 0$: $\int (f + g) = \int f + \int g$; hence for integrable $f, g$ and scalars, the integral is linear.

Proof.

Staircases $\varphi_n \uparrow f$, $\psi_n \uparrow g$ (Theorem 4.9); $\varphi_n + \psi_n \uparrow f + g$; simple additivity (Proposition 4.8) and Theorem 5.1 on all three sequences pass the identity through the limit. The signed case splits into parts, every term finite by integrability. The one-week credit (Remark 4.15) is retired on schedule.

□
Corollary 5.4 (Nonnegative series swap freely).

For measurable $f_k \ge 0$: $\displaystyle \int \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} f_k \, dm = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \int f_k \, dm$ (both sides possibly $\infty$, together).

Proof.

Partial sums climb; Corollary 5.3 prices each; Theorem 5.1 passes the limit.

5.3 The One-Way Valve

Theorem 5.5 (Fatou, 1906).

For measurable $f_n \ge 0$:

\[ \int \liminf_n f_n \, dm \ \le\ \liminf_n \int f_n \, dm . \]
Proof.

$g_n = \inf_{k \ge n} f_k$ is measurable (Theorem 4.6), climbs, and $g_n \uparrow \liminf f_n$ by definition. From $g_n \le f_n$: $\int g_n \le \int f_n$, so $\lim \int g_n \le \liminf \int f_n$ (the left limit exists: MCT’s free half). MCT prices the left limit as $\int \liminf f_n$.

□
Remark 5.6 (Cautions).

Strictness: the spike ($\liminf = 0$; integrals $= 1$). No unsigned valve: the negative spike $-n \mathbf{1}_{(0, 1/n)}$ has $\int \liminf = 0 > -1 = \liminf \int$ read against the claimed direction — the sign gasket is structural. No limsup mirror without a lid: the bump has $\int \limsup = 0 < 1 = \limsup \int$; with an integrable lid $g$, the reverse valve holds ($\limsup \int f_n \le \int \limsup f_n$, by Theorem 5.5 on $g - f_n$ — Problem II.3’s ambit).

5.4 The Crown Jewel

Proposition 5.7 (No lids over the escapes).

Any measurable $g$ dominating all bumps has $g \ge \mathbf{1}_{[0, \infty)}$ a.e.-eventually in the obvious sense and $\int g = \infty$; dominating all spikes forces $g(x) \ge \sup_n n \mathbf{1}_{(0,1/n)}(x)$, which exceeds $\tfrac{1}{2x}$ near nought; dominating all spreads forces $g \ge \tfrac1n$ on $[0, n]$ for all $n$, whence $\int g \ge \sum_n \tfrac1n \cdot 1$-flavoured lower bounds diverge (the desk’s one-line versions: Problem I.4). No integrable lid exists in any of the three galleries.

Proof.

Problem I.4, with the harmonic comparison for the spread and the $1/(2x)$ comparison for the spike (between $1/(k+1)$ and $1/k$ the sup of spikes is at least $k \ge 1/(2x)$).

□
Theorem 5.8 (Dominated convergence — Lebesgue).

Let $f_n$ be measurable, $f_n \to f$ a.e., and $\lvert f_n \rvert \le g$ a.e. for every $n$, with $g$ integrable. Then $f$ is integrable and $\int f_n \, dm \to \int f \, dm$.

Proof.

Patch the null exceptions (the amnesty); assume the hypotheses everywhere. $\lvert f \rvert \le g$ in the limit: $f$ integrable with room ($\int \lvert f \rvert \le \int g < \infty$). The sequences $g + f_n \ge 0$ and $g - f_n \ge 0$ are lawful valve inputs. Fatou on the first:

\[ \int g + \int f \ =\ \int (g + f) \ \le\ \liminf \int (g + f_n) \ =\ \int g + \liminf \int f_n , \]

additivity (Corollary 5.3) expanding both ends; cancel $\int g$ — finite, the lid’s integrability spent, first time: $\int f \le \liminf \int f_n$. Fatou on the second, the same bookkeeping (the second expenditure): $\int g - \int f \le \int g - \limsup \int f_n$, i.e. $\limsup \int f_n \le \int f$. The pinch closes: $\lim \int f_n = \int f$.

□
Corollary 5.9 (The mean-convergence rider).

Under Theorem 5.8’s hypotheses, $\int \lvert f_n - f \rvert \, dm \to 0$.

Proof.

$\lvert f_n - f \rvert \to 0$ a.e., dominated by $2g$; Theorem 5.8 applied to the discrepancies.

□
Corollary 5.10 (Bounded convergence — probability’s workhorse).

On $E$ with $m(E) < \infty$: if $f_n \to f$ a.e. and $\lvert f_n \rvert \le M$ (one constant, all $n$), then $\int f_n \to \int f$.

Proof.

$g = M \mathbf{1}_E$ is integrable on finite ground; the jewel applies.

□

Volume One, Chapter 8’s interchange theorem (uniform convergence on $[a,b]$ licenses the swap) is now two lines: uniform convergence eventually pins $\lvert f_n \rvert \le \lvert f \rvert + 1 \le M+1$ — $f$ bounded as a uniform limit of bounded functions — a constant lid on finite ground; Corollary 5.10. Remark 5.12 (The spread).

$\tfrac1n \mathbf{1}_{[0,n]} \rightrightarrows 0$ uniformly, yet $\int = 1 \not\to 0$: on infinite ground, uniformity neither implies domination nor substitutes for it (the failing line of the old proof: the tube’s cross-section times the ground’s width, $\tfrac1n \cdot n$, refuses to vanish). 5.5 What the Lid Buys

Theorem 5.13 (Continuity in a parameter).

Let $f(x, t)$ be measurable in $x$ for each $t$ in an interval, continuous in $t$ for a.e. $x$, and dominated: $\lvert f(x, t) \rvert \le g(x)$, $g$ integrable, for all $t$. Then $F(t) = \int f(x, t) \, dm(x)$ is continuous.

Proof.

Sequential continuity suffices (Volume One): $t_k \to t$ gives $f(x, t_k) \to f(x, t)$ a.e., dominated by $g$; Theorem 5.8.

□
Theorem 5.14 (Differentiation under the integral sign).

Let $f(x, t)$ be integrable in $x$ for each $t$ in an open interval, differentiable in $t$ for a.e. $x$, with the derivative dominated: $\lvert \partial_t f(x, t) \rvert \le g(x)$, $g$ integrable, for all $t$ in the interval. Then $F(t) = \int f(x, t)\, dm(x)$ is differentiable, and

\[ F'(t) \ =\ \int \partial_t f(x, t) \, dm(x). \]
Proof.

Fix $t$; take any $h_k \to 0$. The quotients $q_k(x) = \bigl( f(x, t + h_k) - f(x, t) \bigr) / h_k$ converge a.e. to $\partial_t f(x, t)$; the mean-value bridge: for each $x$ and $k$, Volume One’s mean value theorem plants a witness $s$ between $t$ and $t + h_k$ with $q_k(x) = \partial_t f(x, s)$, so $\lvert q_k(x) \rvert \le g(x)$ — the derivative’s lid covers every quotient. Theorem 5.8 passes the limit through the integral; linearity identifies the quotient of $F$ with $\int q_k$. Every sequence $h_k$ agreeing, $F'(t)$ exists and equals the display.

□
