The Parliament of Machines
An Audio Course in Distributed Systems
Season complete · twelve episodes · a thirteen-chapter Desk Volume

Twelve evenings on the science of unreliable computers in unreliable company: time and the ordering of events, what mathematics forbids, Paxos derived scar by scar rather than recited, replication and its schools, consistency as a field guide, transactions at a distance, partitioning, the log, and the engineering of failure — conducted throughout as the proceedings of a small and part-time parliament.

Step oneListen

The primary artifact of every course is its EPUB — cut expressly for text-to-speech, with front matter and headings arranged so that nothing is spoken that was not meant to be heard. The recommended arrangement, and the one these were tuned against: open the EPUB in ElevenReader and select the voice Adam Stone, whose delivery suits the register admirably. Failing that, upload the EPUB to Google Play Books and use its Read Aloud — a plainer performance, but a serviceable one.

Step twoThe Desk Volume

Formal statements, complete proofs, diagrams, protocol pseudocode, references, and tiered problems — everything the spoken hour gestures at but cannot carry. Read it after the episode it accompanies; each chapter is that episode's desk companion.

Two editions, and the reason for two: the PDF is the handsome one, typeset properly, and is what you want on a tablet. The browser edition reflows to any screen and is chaptered for reading online. There is deliberately no EPUB of the Desk Volume — the EPUB ecosystem cannot render mathematics with any semblance of decency, and a volume of theorems set in broken glyphs is worse than none.

Context for AI

The entire course — every episode and the whole Desk Volume — flattened into a single Markdown file, mathematics left as raw TeX and diagrams as their source. It is meant for a machine, not a person: hand it to a Claude in a chat or a Project and it will answer questions, set problems, and examine you in the course's own idiom.
