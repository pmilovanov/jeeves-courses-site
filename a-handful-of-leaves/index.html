A Handful of Leaves
An Introduction to Buddhism
In production · ten short evenings · no Desk Volume

Ten evenings of about forty minutes on what the Buddha actually claimed and what became of the claim. The man, told straight, legend and history kept apart; the diagnosis and the physician's format; the anatomy of a self and the argument that there is none to find; the wheel of dependent origination, karma, and rebirth; the treatment, with the practice taught once and honestly; the order and the word, from the last meal to the canon and Ashoka; then the teaching crossing its borders — the Great Vehicle and emptiness, China and Zen, Tibet and the roof of the world, and at last the West, the mindfulness industry, the evidence, and a verdict. Written to be heard on the run or with an infant in one's arms: one idea per evening, said twice.

The Desk Volume

This one has none, by commission: an introduction meant for the ear alone, paced for a listener whose attention is divided. There are no problems to work and no proofs to follow; the discourses it draws on are named aloud, and one book is recommended at the close of each evening for whoever wants the forest rather than the handful.
